The Waterberg’s lower slopes are sourveld, and sourveld needs fire to stay grass. Without it, bush takes over and the grazers leave.

Each autumn the reserve cuts firebreaks on its boundaries. Then, on a written plan, it burns blocks in rotation.

## Cool and early

We burn early in the dry season, while there is still some moisture in the grass and in the soil. A fire on a cool day, without wind, moves slowly and stays low. It takes the old, dead grass and leaves the roots and the seed below the surface. A late, hot fire in the dry months burns deeper and harder and is far more difficult to control.

## What comes after

Within weeks of the first rain, a burnt block is the greenest ground on the reserve. The steenbok, duiker and reedbuck move onto it first. The firebreaks also protect the reserve, the house and our neighbours from wildfires in the dry months.
