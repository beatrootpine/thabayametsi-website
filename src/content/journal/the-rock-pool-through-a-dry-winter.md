The name of the reserve is its hydrology. Rain that falls on the ridge finds its way down through the sandstone and comes out below as springs. Where the springs meet the rock they make pools.

The largest is the rock pool near the house. It holds water through the dry winter, and from June to August it is at its lowest.

## The winter record

We measure the pool on the first of each month. This winter: June <span class="tbc">[level]</span>, July <span class="tbc">[level]</span>, August <span class="tbc">[level]</span>.

The springs and the pool are monitored through the dry season and the boreholes are metered. Nothing is pumped from the kloof. The reserve plans its water against the driest year on record, not the average.

## Who comes to drink

In the dry months the pool draws the reserve to it. Baboons in the late afternoon on their way up to the ridge. Bushbuck at the kloof edge at dusk. Birds all day.

The pool is unfenced, like the springs. Children are in the care of their parents.
