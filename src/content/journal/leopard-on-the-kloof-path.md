Leopard are present on the reserve and rarely seen. What we see instead are their tracks, on the kloof path and on the road, and now and then a kill in a tree.

## Reading the print

A leopard’s print is round, with four toes and no claw marks, because the claws are drawn in when it walks. The main pad has three lobes at the back. A print as wide as it is long, without claw marks, is a cat. A print with claw marks and a narrower shape is more likely to be a dog or a hyena.

Brown hyena use the same paths. Their tracks are on the road most mornings, and the two often cross.

## When to look

The best time to read the paths is at first light, before the sun dries the sand and before anyone has walked on them. Walks on the reserve are with the field guide. The reserve is unfenced to leopard and brown hyena, and walking after dark is not permitted.
