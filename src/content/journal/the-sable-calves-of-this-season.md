The reserve’s sable herd lives in its own fifty-hectare camp on the lower slopes, with its own water and grazing. It is managed for the health and growth of the herd, not for viewing, and that matters most at this time of year.

This season <span class="tbc">[number]</span> calves have been born in the camp, the first on <span class="tbc">[date]</span>.

A sable calf spends its first weeks lying hidden in long grass, away from the herd. Its mother comes back to it to feed it and then leaves again. For those weeks we keep the vehicle well back from the parts of the camp where the cows have been calving, and we count the calves from a distance.

The calves are a light fawn colour when they are born. They join the herd once they can keep up with it, and from then on they are easier to see when the herd comes to water in the late afternoon.

Guests of The Maravi visit the camp with the field guide. The animals are not habituated to vehicles and are not approached on foot.
